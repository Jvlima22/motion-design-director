\# Shot Vocabulary



\## Purpose



This document defines a reusable shot vocabulary for professional motion design,

especially SaaS, apps, software, AI products and technology launches.



A shot is not simply a camera angle.



A shot is a communication unit.



Every shot should answer:



WHAT MUST THE VIEWER UNDERSTAND?



WHAT SHOULD THEY LOOK AT?



WHY IS THIS SHOT HERE?



WHAT CHANGES BEFORE THE NEXT SHOT?



Use this vocabulary before choosing implementation details.



\---



\# 1. Shot Selection Framework



Choose shots in this order:



MESSAGE

→ STORY FUNCTION

→ HERO

→ SHOT TYPE

→ COMPOSITION

→ CAMERA

→ MOTION

→ TRANSITION

→ SOUND

→ IMPLEMENTATION



Do not begin with:



"Let's add a cool animation."



Begin with:



"What shot communicates this idea best?"



\---



\# 2. Shot Families



Use five primary families:



ESTABLISH

→ introduce world/product



EXPLAIN

→ communicate feature/process



FOCUS

→ isolate important detail



TRANSFORM

→ communicate change/result



RESOLVE

→ provide payoff/brand/CTA



A strong product film usually combines several families.



\---



\# 3. Product Establishing Shot



\## Purpose



Introduce the product and establish visual context.



\## Best For



\- opening

\- product introduction

\- returning from abstract sequence

\- establishing dashboard/app environment



\## Visual Structure



PRODUCT

as primary hero



ENVIRONMENT

as supporting context



BRAND

present but restrained



\## Camera



Usually:



\- static

\- slow push

\- controlled pull-out

\- mild perspective



\## Motion



Reveal major visual layers progressively.



Avoid animating every UI element individually.



\## Sound



\- atmosphere

\- soft rise

\- restrained impact



\## Transition Out



Move toward the first important feature.



\---



\# 4. Hero Product Shot



\## Purpose



Make the product feel desirable and important.



\## Best For



\- opening

\- midpoint payoff

\- closing hero

\- launch moment



\## Visual Structure



One dominant product object.



Use:



\- negative space

\- controlled lighting

\- depth

\- strong silhouette

\- restrained supporting elements



\## Camera



\- slow push

\- mild orbit

\- controlled parallax

\- pull-out reveal



\## Motion



Minimal.



Let camera, lighting and environmental motion provide life.



\## Avoid



Busy interface interaction.



Hero shots sell perception before functionality.



\---



\# 5. Device Hero



\## Purpose



Present an application inside a physical device.



\## Best For



\- mobile apps

\- wearables

\- desktop products

\- cross-platform products



\## Visual Structure



DEVICE

→ SCREEN

→ FEATURE



\## Variations



FRONTAL HERO



THREE-QUARTER HERO



FLOATING DEVICE



MULTI-DEVICE



DEVICE ARRAY



MACRO DEVICE



\## Camera



Use stronger perspective during introduction.



Reduce perspective when screen readability becomes important.



\---



\# 6. UI Hero



\## Purpose



Present the actual interface as the main visual object.



\## Best For



\- SaaS

\- dashboards

\- desktop apps

\- web platforms



\## Visual Structure



UI dominates frame.



Supporting environment remains secondary.



\## Camera



Prefer:



\- frontal

\- slight perspective

\- slow push



\## Motion



Use:



\- progressive reveal

\- controlled depth

\- feature highlighting

\- subtle ambient motion



\---



\# 7. Interface Establishing Shot



\## Purpose



Allow viewer to understand interface architecture before focusing on details.



\## Show



\- navigation

\- major content areas

\- hierarchy

\- primary product context



Do not immediately zoom into details before viewers understand where they are.



\---



\# 8. Feature Focus Shot



\## Purpose



Isolate one product feature.



\## Pattern



CONTEXT

→ ISOLATION

→ FEATURE

→ ACTION

→ RESULT



\## Isolation Methods



\- dim surrounding UI

\- crop/reframe

\- camera push

\- scale feature

\- blur surroundings

\- depth separation

\- spotlight

\- color emphasis



\## Best For



Feature launches and product demos.



\---



\# 9. Macro Feature Shot



\## Purpose



Make a small UI interaction feel important.



\## Best For



\- toggle

\- button

\- metric

\- AI response

\- notification

\- chart point

\- control

\- status



\## Camera



Macro push or crop.



\## Motion



Keep surrounding movement minimal.



\## Sound



Small tactile sound can reinforce interaction.



\---



\# 10. UI Close-Up



\## Purpose



Show detailed interface behavior clearly.



Unlike Macro Feature,

the goal may be comprehension rather than dramatic emphasis.



Use for:



\- forms

\- settings

\- filters

\- menus

\- tables

\- editor interactions



Prefer frontal composition.



\---



\# 11. Cursor Interaction Shot



\## Purpose



Demonstrate how a human interacts with the product.



\## Pattern



CURSOR ENTERS

→ TARGET ACQUIRES ATTENTION

→ CURSOR APPROACHES

→ CLICK

→ UI RESPONDS

→ RESULT HOLDS



\## Rules



Cursor should not wander.



Movement should appear intentional.



Camera should usually settle before important cursor interaction.



\---



\# 12. Click Payoff Shot



\## Purpose



Turn a simple click into a narrative event.



Example:



CLICK

→ SYSTEM REACTS

→ FEATURE ACTIVATES

→ RESULT APPEARS



Useful for:



\- automation

\- AI generation

\- publishing

\- sending

\- payment

\- creation

\- deployment



The click itself is not the hero.



The consequence is.



\---



\# 13. Progressive Reveal



\## Purpose



Introduce complexity gradually.



Pattern:



BASE

→ PRIMARY ELEMENT

→ SUPPORTING ELEMENTS

→ DETAILS

→ FULL SYSTEM



Useful for complex SaaS interfaces.



Avoid dumping the complete dashboard onto screen instantly.



\---



\# 14. UI Assembly Shot



\## Purpose



Show interface pieces assembling into a complete product.



Pattern:



NAV

\+

CARDS

\+

CHARTS

\+

CONTROLS

→

COMPLETE UI



Useful for:



\- product introduction

\- design-system story

\- feature ecosystem

\- product build-up



Keep assembly hierarchical.



Do not make every component arrive from a random direction.



\---



\# 15. UI Explosion Shot



\## Purpose



Separate interface layers/components spatially to reveal structure.



Pattern:



COMPLETE UI

→ COMPONENTS SEPARATE

→ RELATIONSHIPS BECOME VISIBLE



Useful for:



\- architecture

\- modular products

\- feature ecosystems

\- explaining UI composition



Can use:



\- Z-depth

\- horizontal separation

\- radial separation

\- layered decomposition



Avoid meaningless explosions.



Every separated component should reveal something.



\---



\# 16. UI Decomposition Shot



\## Purpose



Break a complex product into understandable functional groups.



Different from UI Explosion:



Explosion emphasizes spatial spectacle.



Decomposition emphasizes explanation.



Pattern:



PRODUCT

→ MODULES

→ FUNCTION

→ REASSEMBLE



\---



\# 17. Floating UI Shot



\## Purpose



Place UI components in dimensional space.



Useful for:



\- hero sequences

\- ecosystem shots

\- integrations

\- feature collections

\- AI interfaces



Use depth hierarchy:



BACKGROUND SUPPORT



MIDGROUND CONTEXT



FOREGROUND HERO



Avoid "floating card soup."



Every card needs a relationship to the narrative.



\---



\# 18. Spatial Dashboard Shot



\## Purpose



Transform a flat dashboard into a dimensional environment.



Possible structure:



BACKGROUND

dashboard foundation



MIDGROUND

supporting panels



FOREGROUND

hero metric / feature



Useful for cinematic SaaS moments.



Maintain recognizability of the original interface.



\---



\# 19. Layer Stack Shot



\## Purpose



Explain system architecture through stacked visual layers.



Example:



USER

↓

INTERFACE

↓

AUTOMATION

↓

AI

↓

DATA



Can be horizontal, vertical or Z-depth.



Useful for technical products.



\---



\# 20. Data Flow Shot



\## Purpose



Show information moving between systems.



Possible visual language:



NODE

→ LINE

→ PACKET

→ DESTINATION

→ RESULT



Use for:



\- integrations

\- automation

\- APIs

\- AI pipelines

\- CRM

\- finance

\- analytics



Movement should show direction and causality.



\---



\# 21. Connection Shot



\## Purpose



Show relationships between multiple products, services or modules.



Visual forms:



\- lines

\- arcs

\- paths

\- beams

\- pulses

\- moving particles

\- expanding network



Avoid decorative connection lines that communicate nothing.



\---



\# 22. Integration Shot



\## Purpose



Show external services connecting to the product.



Possible composition:



PRODUCT

at center



INTEGRATIONS

around or beside



CONNECTIONS

between



Useful for:



\- APIs

\- automation platforms

\- CRM

\- payment systems

\- AI orchestration



Brand logos should remain readable.



\---



\# 23. Ecosystem Reveal



\## Purpose



Show that a feature belongs to a larger system.



Pattern:



FEATURE

→ MODULE

→ PRODUCT

→ ECOSYSTEM



Camera often pulls out.



Useful for product breadth.



\---



\# 24. Architecture Reveal



\## Purpose



Explain invisible technical architecture visually.



Possible layers:



INPUT

PROCESS

INTELLIGENCE

DATA

OUTPUT



Prefer semantic structure over arbitrary sci-fi graphics.



\---



\# 25. AI Processing Shot



\## Purpose



Visualize an invisible AI operation.



Possible metaphors:



\- branching

\- convergence

\- transformation

\- semantic linking

\- classification

\- synthesis

\- contextual retrieval

\- progressive refinement



Avoid default:



random glowing particles.



The metaphor should match the actual operation.



\---



\# 26. AI Input → Output Shot



\## Purpose



Communicate AI value clearly.



Pattern:



INPUT

→ PROCESS

→ OUTPUT



Example:



PROMPT

→ AI PROCESS

→ GENERATED RESULT



Keep process section short unless architecture itself is important.



\---



\# 27. Before / After Shot



\## Purpose



Communicate transformation.



Pattern:



BEFORE

→ TRANSITION

→ AFTER



Useful for:



\- productivity

\- design tools

\- optimization

\- automation

\- AI

\- analytics



Make the difference visually obvious.



\---



\# 28. Problem → Solution Shot



\## Purpose



Turn product value into visual storytelling.



Pattern:



PROBLEM STATE

→ FRICTION

→ PRODUCT ACTION

→ SOLUTION STATE



This is stronger than showing features without context.



\---



\# 29. Chaos → Order Shot



\## Purpose



Show organization or automation.



Pattern:



SCATTERED

MESSY

DISCONNECTED



→



SYSTEM ACTION



→



ORDERED

CONNECTED

CLEAR



Useful for:



\- CRM

\- project management

\- AI organization

\- finance

\- automation



\---



\# 30. Manual → Automated Shot



\## Purpose



Show reduction of repetitive work.



Possible structure:



MULTIPLE MANUAL ACTIONS



collapse into:



ONE AUTOMATED FLOW



Useful for SaaS automation narratives.



\---



\# 31. Data → Insight Shot



\## Purpose



Show raw information becoming useful understanding.



Pattern:



RAW DATA

→ PROCESSING

→ VISUALIZATION

→ INSIGHT



Useful for:



\- analytics

\- dashboards

\- AI

\- finance

\- CRM



\---



\# 32. Metric Hero Shot



\## Purpose



Make a result measurable.



Examples:



+37%



2.4× faster



10h saved



1,240 leads



Use large stable typography.



Avoid excessive animation.



Credibility matters more than spectacle.



\---



\# 33. Counter Shot



\## Purpose



Show numeric progression.



Use when change itself matters.



Possible:



0 → 10K



24 → 86%



1 → 100



Do not animate every number in every dashboard.



\---



\# 34. Chart Reveal



\## Purpose



Communicate change visually.



Pattern:



AXIS / CONTEXT

→ DATA

→ IMPORTANT POINT

→ INSIGHT



Highlight the meaningful data,

not every point.



\---



\# 35. Comparison Shot



\## Purpose



Compare:



\- before/after

\- old/new

\- plan A/B

\- manual/automated

\- competitor approaches

\- states



Possible structures:



SPLIT SCREEN



SIDE BY SIDE



SLIDER



MORPH



STACKED STATES



Keep comparison criteria visually consistent.



\---



\# 36. Split-Screen Shot



\## Purpose



Show parallel states or processes.



Useful for:



\- comparison

\- synchronization

\- cross-platform

\- collaboration

\- before/after



Avoid overcrowding both sides.



\---



\# 37. Multi-Device Shot



\## Purpose



Show product availability across platforms.



Possible:



PHONE

\+

TABLET

\+

DESKTOP

\+

WATCH



Do not make every device equally dominant.



Choose one hero.



\---



\# 38. Cross-Platform Continuity Shot



\## Purpose



Show the same action continuing between devices.



Example:



PHONE ACTION

→ DESKTOP CONTINUES

→ WATCH CONFIRMS



Useful for ecosystem storytelling.



\---



\# 39. Notification Shot



\## Purpose



Communicate system response or real-time value.



Possible:



event

→ notification enters

→ user sees result



Keep notification readable.



Avoid excessive notification stacks unless demonstrating volume.



\---



\# 40. Workflow Shot



\## Purpose



Explain sequential process.



Pattern:



STEP 1

→ STEP 2

→ STEP 3

→ RESULT



Possible visual forms:



\- connected nodes

\- cards

\- timeline

\- spatial path

\- progressive UI



Show direction clearly.



\---



\# 41. Timeline Shot



\## Purpose



Communicate progression over time.



Useful for:



\- workflow

\- onboarding

\- project status

\- automation

\- history



Motion direction should remain consistent.



\---



\# 42. Node Graph Shot



\## Purpose



Show relationships and branching.



Useful for:



\- AI agents

\- automation

\- system architecture

\- integrations

\- workflows



Hierarchy must remain visible.



Avoid turning graph into visual spaghetti.



\---



\# 43. Search / Discovery Shot



\## Purpose



Show product helping user find something.



Pattern:



QUERY

→ SEARCH

→ FILTER / PROCESS

→ RESULT



Useful for:



\- lead generation

\- AI search

\- databases

\- marketplaces

\- knowledge tools



\---



\# 44. Generation Shot



\## Purpose



Show content being created.



Pattern:



INPUT

→ PROCESS

→ OUTPUT BUILDS

→ FINAL RESULT



Useful for generative AI.



Avoid fake loading that lasts longer than narrative requires.



\---



\# 45. Transformation Shot



\## Purpose



Show one visual state becoming another.



Examples:



TEXT

→ VIDEO



DATA

→ CHART



LEAD

→ CUSTOMER



PROMPT

→ DESIGN



Useful for product value communication.



\---



\# 46. Morph Shot



\## Purpose



Create continuity by transforming one object into another.



Works best when objects share:



\- shape

\- position

\- semantic relationship

\- color

\- motion direction



Avoid morphing unrelated objects purely for spectacle.



\---



\# 47. Match Cut



\## Purpose



Connect two scenes through visual similarity.



Match:



\- shape

\- position

\- scale

\- direction

\- color

\- action



Example:



circular chart

→ circular logo



This often feels more sophisticated than an effect-heavy transition.



\---



\# 48. Match Movement Shot



\## Purpose



Continue motion through a cut.



Example:



CARD MOVES RIGHT

→ CUT

→ NEXT OBJECT CONTINUES RIGHT



Creates continuity and speed.



\---



\# 49. Push-Through Shot



\## Purpose



Use an interface element as a portal to the next shot.



Pattern:



CAMERA APPROACHES

→ ELEMENT FILLS FRAME

→ NEXT SCENE



Useful for:



overview → feature



feature → detail



product → system



\---



\# 50. Occlusion Shot



\## Purpose



Use foreground movement to hide a cut.



Possible occluders:



\- UI panel

\- device

\- card

\- text

\- geometry



Creates seamless scene transitions.



\---



\# 51. Zoom-Out Reveal



\## Purpose



Begin with detail and reveal surprising context.



Pattern:



MACRO DETAIL

→ PULL OUT

→ FEATURE

→ PRODUCT

→ SYSTEM



Useful for establishing scale.



\---



\# 52. Zoom-In Discovery



\## Purpose



Move from broad context to important detail.



Pattern:



SYSTEM

→ PRODUCT

→ FEATURE

→ ACTION



Useful for product explanations.



\---



\# 53. Scroll Journey



\## Purpose



Represent navigation through a long interface or story.



Use carefully.



Do not simply record a webpage scrolling.



Add hierarchy through:



\- pacing

\- camera

\- crop

\- pauses

\- feature emphasis



\---



\# 54. Page / Screen Transition



\## Purpose



Show navigation between product screens.



Possible methods:



\- slide

\- push

\- mask

\- shared element

\- morph

\- spatial move

\- native UI transition



Prefer transitions that respect product behavior.



\---



\# 55. Shared Element Transition



\## Purpose



Use one persistent element to connect screens.



Example:



CARD

on dashboard



→ expands



→ becomes detail page



This preserves spatial understanding.



\---



\# 56. Dimensional Transition



\## Purpose



Move between scenes through Z-space.



Possible:



foreground passes camera



camera travels through layer



UI panels separate



new environment appears behind



Useful for cinematic product films.



\---



\# 57. Typography Hero Shot



\## Purpose



Let the message become the visual hero.



Useful for:



\- opening statement

\- positioning

\- section transition

\- payoff

\- campaign line



Keep supporting visuals restrained.



\---



\# 58. Kinetic Type Shot



\## Purpose



Use typography motion to communicate rhythm or meaning.



Possible:



\- word replacement

\- scale emphasis

\- mask reveal

\- tracking expansion

\- directional movement

\- semantic morph



Avoid animating every letter independently without reason.



\---



\# 59. Statement → Product Shot



\## Purpose



Connect marketing promise directly to product proof.



Pattern:



CLAIM

→ TRANSITION

→ PRODUCT FEATURE PROVES CLAIM



This is highly useful in SaaS launch videos.



\---



\# 60. Logo Reveal



\## Purpose



Resolve brand identity.



Possible families:



ASSEMBLY



MASK



STROKE



PARTICLE



LIGHT



MORPH



GEOMETRIC



UI-TO-LOGO



Keep logo geometry recognizable.



\---



\# 61. Product → Logo Transition



\## Purpose



End product story by transforming interface structure into brand.



Possible:



UI elements collapse

→ logo



hero color converges

→ logo



product window shrinks

→ logo container



Useful for memorable endings.

\# 62. Logo → Product Transition



\## Purpose



Start from brand and enter product world.



Pattern:



LOGO

→ interface emerges

LOGO

→ camera enters

→ product appears



Useful for branded openings.



\---



\# 63. CTA Shot



\## Purpose



Tell viewer what to do next.



Possible content:



START FREE



DOWNLOAD



TRY NOW



BOOK DEMO



LEARN MORE



Visual hierarchy:



CTA

→ BRAND

→ SUPPORTING INFO



Do not overload closing frame.



\---



\# 64. Brand Resolve



\## Purpose



Give the film a confident ending.



Possible:



LOGO

\+

TAGLINE



or:



PRODUCT

\+

LOGO

\+

CTA



Hold long enough to register.



\---



\# 65. Stillness Shot



\## Purpose



Create contrast and confidence.



Useful after:



\- fast sequence

\- complex workflow

\- dramatic reveal

\- high-energy montage



Stillness is part of motion design vocabulary.



\---



\# 66. Breathing Shot



\## Purpose



Allow the viewer to process information.



May contain only:



\- subtle environment

\- slow camera

\- minimal UI motion



Useful between information-dense sections.



\---



\# 67. Montage Shot



\## Purpose



Show breadth quickly.



Possible sequence:



FEATURE

→ FEATURE

→ FEATURE

→ FEATURE



Keep common visual logic:



\- same direction

\- same framing family

\- same rhythm

\- same transition grammar



Montage should feel designed, not random.



\---



\# 68. Feature Cascade



\## Purpose



Reveal multiple capabilities in rapid succession.



Pattern:



FEATURE A

→ FEATURE B

→ FEATURE C

→ PRODUCT PAYOFF



Useful for mid-video acceleration.



\---



\# 69. Interface Mosaic



\## Purpose



Show product breadth using multiple UI fragments.



Useful for:



\- feature-rich products

\- ecosystem overview

\- montage



Maintain one dominant tile.



Avoid equal visual weight everywhere.



\---



\# 70. Infinite Canvas Shot



\## Purpose



Suggest a product/system larger than the visible frame.



Camera navigates through:



\- cards

\- nodes

\- workflows

\- modules

\- data



Useful for automation and AI platforms.



Requires strong spatial organization.



\---



\# 71. World-Building Shot



\## Purpose



Transform product language into a visual environment.



Examples:



UI cards become architecture.



Data lines become pathways.



Nodes become spatial systems.



Use for high-concept launch films.



Do not lose connection to actual product.



\---



\# 72. Abstract Metaphor Shot



\## Purpose



Explain an idea that cannot be shown directly.



Examples:



speed

→ compressed distance



connection

→ converging paths



intelligence

→ adaptive network



automation

→ self-moving system



Return to product proof afterward.



\---



\# 73. Proof Shot



\## Purpose



Support a claim with visible evidence.



Possible proof:



\- actual UI

\- metric

\- workflow

\- result

\- customer outcome

\- output



Marketing claims become stronger when followed by proof.



\---



\# 74. Result Shot



\## Purpose



Show what the user receives.



Examples:



\- generated design

\- booked meeting

\- completed workout

\- sales insight

\- payment

\- report

\- automated action



Give result enough screen time to register.



\---



\# 75. Payoff Shot



\## Purpose



Resolve the narrative promise.



This may combine:



RESULT

\+

PRODUCT

\+

BRAND



It should feel more satisfying than the setup.

\# 76. Shot Energy Levels



Classify each shot:



LEVEL 1 — STILL



LEVEL 2 — SUBTLE



LEVEL 3 — ACTIVE



LEVEL 4 — DYNAMIC



LEVEL 5 — HERO / IMPACT



Do not make every shot Level 5.



Energy contrast creates rhythm.



\---



\# 77. Shot Density Levels



LOW



one hero

large negative space



MEDIUM



hero + supporting information



HIGH



multiple meaningful elements



VERY HIGH



montage/system overview



Alternate density intentionally.

\# 78. Shot Duration



Useful starting ranges:



MICRO SHOT

0.4–1.0s



FAST FEATURE

1–2s



STANDARD PRODUCT SHOT

2–4s



EXPLANATION

3–6s



HERO

3–8s

\# 80. Shot Exit



Before designing entry,

know how the shot exits.



A good shot often contains the seed of the next shot.



Example:



hero card moves toward camera

→ becomes transition occluder

→ reveals next scene



This improves continuity.

\# 89. Avoid Repetition



Do not use the same shot grammar repeatedly.



Bad:



push into card

→ push into card

→ push into card

→ push into card



Better:



ESTABLISH

→ PUSH

→ STATIC INTERACTION

→ DATA FLOW

→ MACRO

→ PULL OUT

→ PAYOFF



Variation should still belong to one visual language.



\---



\# 90. Avoid Effect-Driven Shot Selection



Do not choose a shot because:



"this transition looks cool."



Choose it because:



"this transition preserves the relationship between these ideas."



Story determines vocabulary.



\---



\# 91. Avoid Dashboard Dump



Do not show the entire product and expect viewers to discover the feature.



Use:



CONTEXT

→ FOCUS

→ ACTION

→ RESULT



\---



\# 92. Avoid Floating Card Soup



Floating UI is not automatically cinematic.



Every floating component should have:



ROLE

\+

HIERARCHY

\+

SPATIAL RELATIONSHIP



Remove cards that do not contribute.



\---



\# 93. Avoid Fake Complexity



More layers do not automatically communicate a more advanced product.



Use complexity only when the product concept requires it.



\---



\# 94. Avoid Constant Motion



If everything moves:



nothing feels important.



Use stillness and holds.



\---



\# 95. Avoid Camera Dependence



A weak composition cannot be fixed by constantly moving the camera.



First design the frame.



Then move the camera.



\---



\# 96. Avoid Transition Dependence



A sequence should still make narrative sense with simple cuts.



Transitions improve continuity.



They should not create the story by themselves.



\---



\# 97. Shot Review



For every shot ask:

\# 99. Creative Director Rule



Never output a list of random visual ideas.



When proposing a shot,

connect it to:



MESSAGE

→ SHOT

→ REASON

→ CAMERA

→ MOTION

→ TRANSITION

→ SOUND



Example:



MESSAGE:

AI automatically qualifies incoming leads.



SHOT:

Data Flow → AI Processing → Result.



REASON:

The user needs to understand causality.



CAMERA:

Slow follow along the data path.



MOTION:

Lead card enters, passes through qualification nodes,

then changes state.



TRANSITION:

Camera follows qualified lead into CRM interface.



SOUND:

Subtle pulses at processing nodes,

one confirmation sound at result.

\# 100. Golden Rule



A professional product video is not a collection of cool animations.



It is a sequence of intentionally chosen shots where every visual decision

helps the viewer understand, feel or remember something.



Choose the shot for the message.



Choose the motion for the shot.



Choose the effect for the motion.



Choose the technology last.
