\# Transition System



\## Purpose



Define a professional transition language for motion design, SaaS, apps,

software, AI products and technology films.



A transition is not decoration placed between two shots.



A transition is the visual relationship that connects:



SHOT A

→ CHANGE

→ SHOT B



The best transition often feels inevitable.



\---



\# 1. Transition Decision Framework



Before choosing a transition identify:



SHOT A

→ what is the viewer looking at?



SHOT B

→ what must they look at next?



RELATIONSHIP

→ what connects these ideas?



DIRECTION

→ where is visual energy moving?



HERO

→ which element can carry continuity?



CAMERA

→ can camera movement create the transition?



UI

→ can product behavior create the transition?



SOUND

→ what should reinforce the change?



Then choose the transition.



\---



\# 2. Transition Priority



Prefer transition logic in this order:



1\. NARRATIVE CONTINUITY



2\. SHARED ELEMENT



3\. MOTION CONTINUITY



4\. SPATIAL CONTINUITY



5\. CAMERA CONTINUITY



6\. UI-NATIVE CONTINUITY



7\. GRAPHIC CONTINUITY



8\. EFFECT-BASED TRANSITION



Do not begin with transition presets.

\---



\# 4. Hard Cut



The simplest transition.



SHOT A

→ CUT

→ SHOT B



Use when:



\- composition changes clearly

\- rhythm needs speed

\- action already creates continuity

\- transition effect would distract

\- information is more important than spectacle



A professional sequence does not need an effect between every shot.



\---



\# 5. Rhythm Cut



Cut according to:



\- musical beat

\- motion landing

\- spoken phrase

\- product action

\- visual accent



Useful for:



\- montage

\- feature cascade

\- launch sequences



Do not cut mechanically on every beat.



\---



\# 6. Action Cut



Cut during or immediately after an action.



Example:



CLICK

→ CUT

→ RESULT



or:



CARD STARTS MOVING

→ CUT

→ CARD CONTINUES MOVING



Action hides the discontinuity.



\---



\# 7. Match Cut



Connect shots through visual similarity.



Match:



SHAPE



POSITION



SCALE



COLOR



DIRECTION



ACTION



COMPOSITION



SEMANTIC MEANING



Example:



circular chart

→ circular logo



\---



\# 8. Shape Match



SHOT A ends with a recognizable shape.



SHOT B begins with a similar shape.



Examples:



circle

→ logo mark



card

→ device



node

→ avatar



graph point

→ location marker



Shape continuity makes the cut feel designed.



\---



\# 9. Position Match



Keep hero element in approximately the same screen position across shots.



Useful for:



\- feature changes

\- product states

\- comparison

\- device transitions



Viewer attention does not need to relocate.



\---



\# 10. Scale Match



End one shot with an object at a scale similar to the next shot's hero.



Useful for:



\- macro transitions

\- UI → device

\- icon → product element



\---



\# 11. Color Match



Use dominant color to connect scenes.



Example:



hero blue card

→ frame fills blue

→ next scene emerges from same blue field



Useful when geometry differs but brand continuity matters.



\---



\# 12. Semantic Match



Connect different objects because they represent the same concept.



Example:



LEAD CARD

→ CRM CONTACT



DATA PACKET

→ ANALYTICS POINT



AI NODE

→ GENERATED RESULT



Semantic transitions are especially powerful in product storytelling.



\---



\# 13. Match Movement



Continue motion direction through the cut.



Example:



SHOT A:

object moves right



SHOT B:

new object continues right



Maintain:



DIRECTION

VELOCITY

ENERGY



This can make a simple cut feel cinematic.



\---



\# 14. Motion Vector Continuity



Think of motion as a vector.



SHOT A:

→



SHOT B:

→



Good continuity.



SHOT A:

→



SHOT B:

←



creates deliberate opposition.



Do not reverse direction accidentally.



\---



\# 15. Shared Element Transition



One object persists across both scenes.



Pattern:



SHOT A

→ SHARED ELEMENT CHANGES

→ SHOT B



Examples:



CARD

→ DETAIL VIEW



BUTTON

→ MODAL



IMAGE

→ FULLSCREEN



NODE

→ WORKFLOW



DEVICE SCREEN

→ FULL UI



One of the strongest product-video transition families.



\---



\# 16. Shared Element Expansion



SMALL ELEMENT

→ EXPANDS

→ BECOMES NEXT SCENE



Useful for:



\- dashboard card → detail

\- thumbnail → content

\- chart → analytics view

\- notification → conversation



Preserve identity.



\---



\# 17. Shared Element Collapse



Reverse:



FULL VIEW

→ COLLAPSES

→ RETURNS TO CONTEXT



Useful for returning from feature detail.



\---



\# 18. Shared Element Morph



Element changes form while retaining identity.



Possible continuity cues:



\- same color

\- same position

\- same content

\- same icon

\- same shape family



Useful when product states differ structurally.



\---



\# 19. UI-Native Transition



Use actual product behavior as transition.



Examples:



\- modal opens

\- card expands

\- sidebar slides

\- tab changes

\- page navigates

\- dropdown opens

\- panel collapses

\- screen scrolls



This often creates the most credible SaaS transitions.



\---



\# 20. Card Expansion Transition



Pattern:



CARD SELECTED

→ CARD MOVES FORWARD

→ EXPANDS

→ DETAIL FILLS FRAME



Useful for feature focus.



\---



\# 21. Card Collapse Transition



DETAIL

→ SHRINKS

→ CARD RETURNS

→ DASHBOARD REAPPEARS



Useful for restoring product context.



\---



\# 22. Modal Transition



BACKGROUND

→ DIM

→ MODAL ENTERS



The modal itself may become the next scene.



Useful for:



\- settings

\- AI interaction

\- form

\- confirmation



\---



\# 23. Sidebar Transition



Sidebar can act as an occluder.



Pattern:



SIDEBAR MOVES

→ FILLS FRAME

→ NEXT SCENE APPEARS



Useful because transition originates from actual UI.



\---



\# 24. Tab Transition



Pattern:



ACTIVE INDICATOR MOVES

→ CONTENT CHANGES

→ NEW TAB SETTLES



Can bridge related feature shots.



\---



\# 25. Scroll Transition



Use scrolling to reach next narrative region.



Pattern:



CONTENT MOVES

→ HERO PASSES

→ NEXT FEATURE SETTLES



Avoid endless webpage recording.



\---



\# 26. Device-to-UI Transition



Pattern:



DEVICE HERO

→ CAMERA APPROACHES SCREEN

→ SCREEN FILLS FRAME

→ DEVICE FRAME DISAPPEARS

→ FULL UI



One of the strongest app-film transitions.



\---



\# 27. UI-to-Device Transition



Reverse:



FULL UI

→ SCALE REDUCES

→ DEVICE FRAME APPEARS

→ CAMERA PULLS OUT



Useful for resolving a feature sequence.



\---



\# 28. Camera Transition



Use camera movement itself to connect shots.



Possible:



PUSH



PULL



PAN



FOLLOW



ORBIT



WHIP



TRAVEL THROUGH



Camera should have the same narrative intent across transition.



\---



\# 29. Push Transition



Camera pushes toward hero.



Hero fills frame.



Next scene begins from that region.



Useful for:



OVERVIEW

→ DETAIL



PRODUCT

→ FEATURE



DEVICE

→ UI



\---



\# 30. Pull Transition



Camera pulls away to reveal new context.



Useful for:



FEATURE

→ PRODUCT



PRODUCT

→ ECOSYSTEM



DETAIL

→ SYSTEM



\---



\# 31. Pan Transition



Camera pans toward new information.



Possible:



SHOT A exits left



SHOT B enters from right



Maintain directional continuity.



\---



\# 32. Follow Transition



Camera follows a moving object into next scene.



Example:



DATA PACKET

→ CAMERA FOLLOWS

→ ENTERS CRM

→ NEXT SHOT



Excellent for workflow narratives.



\---



\# 33. Whip Transition



Fast camera movement creates blur that hides cut.



Use for:



\- energetic montage

\- major directional shift

\- rapid feature sequence



Avoid repeated whip transitions.



Premium SaaS rarely needs constant whipping.



\---



\# 34. Travel-Through Transition



Camera physically travels through:



\- screen

\- card

\- node

\- portal

\- device

\- geometry



Useful for dimensional storytelling.



The destination should relate to the object entered.



\---



\# 35. Spatial Transition



Scenes exist within one larger spatial environment.



Camera moves from one region to another.



Example:



FEATURE A

← camera travels →

FEATURE B



Useful for:



\- infinite canvas

\- automation

\- AI systems

\- product ecosystems



\---



\# 36. Z-Space Transition



Use depth axis.



Possible:



foreground moves past camera



camera moves through layers



next scene exists behind current scene



Useful for dimensional UI.



\---



\# 37. Foreground Pass



A foreground element crosses camera.



During occlusion:



change scene.



Then reveal next shot.



Possible occluders:



CARD



DEVICE



TEXT



GEOMETRY



UI PANEL



\---



\# 38. Occlusion Transition



General pattern:



SHOT A

→ OCCLUDER FILLS FRAME

→ HIDDEN CUT

→ OCCLUDER LEAVES

→ SHOT B



Very useful because it does not require obvious digital effects.



\---



\# 39. Natural Occlusion



Prefer objects already present in scene.



Examples:



device edge



sidebar



modal



card



headline



This makes transition feel motivated.



\---



\# 40. Mask Transition



Use a shape to reveal next shot.



Possible masks:



LOGO



TEXT



CARD



CIRCLE



DEVICE SCREEN



UI WINDOW



GRAPHIC SHAPE



Prefer meaningful masks.



\---



\# 41. Logo Mask Transition



Camera or content passes through brand mark.



Useful for:



\- branded section transition

\- opening

\- closing



Do not overuse logo as a portal.



\---



\# 42. Text Mask Transition



Large typography reveals next scene.



Useful for:



\- section title

\- campaign statement

\- feature category



Text must remain readable before becoming transition.



\---



\# 43. UI Mask Transition



Use actual UI shape as mask.



Examples:



CARD

→ NEXT FEATURE



CHART

→ DATA SCENE



IMAGE

→ FULL CONTENT



Strong for product films.



\---



\# 44. Morph Transition



One visual state transforms into another.



Works best when scenes share:



\- shape

\- structure

\- meaning

\- color

\- position



Morphing unrelated objects usually feels arbitrary.



\---



\# 45. Geometric Morph



Shape A becomes Shape B.



Useful for:



\- logo

\- icon

\- product geometry

\- abstract transitions



Maintain clean silhouettes.



\---



\# 46. Semantic Morph



One concept becomes another.



Example:



LEAD

→ CUSTOMER



PROMPT

→ RESULT



DATA

→ INSIGHT



This can communicate product value directly.



\---



\# 47. UI Morph



Interface state transforms smoothly.



Examples:



CARD

→ DETAIL



SEARCH

→ RESULTS



DRAFT

→ PUBLISHED



PROMPT

→ RESPONSE



Preserve recognizable anchors.



\---



\# 48. Data Morph



Data visualization changes form.



Examples:



RAW NUMBERS

→ CHART



POINTS

→ LINE



LEADS

→ PIPELINE



Useful for analytics stories.



\---



\# 49. Light Transition



Use light as continuity mechanism.



Possible:



FLASH



LIGHT SWEEP



GLOW EXPANSION



BRIGHT FIELD



LENS-LIKE PASS



Use carefully.



Light should not become default transition.



\---



\# 50. Light Sweep Transition



Bright sweep crosses frame.



At peak:



change scene.



Useful for:



\- premium

\- device

\- logo

\- polished product hero



\---



\# 51. Glow Expansion



Hero glow expands until it fills frame.



Next scene emerges from same color/light.



Useful for:



\- AI activation

\- logo

\- energy

\- brand transition



\---



\# 52. Flash Transition



Very short luminance event hides cut.



Useful for:



\- impact

\- photography metaphor

\- high-energy montage



Avoid frequent flashing.



\---



\# 53. Blur Transition



Use blur generated by motion or focus.



Possible:



MOTION BLUR



DIRECTIONAL BLUR



ZOOM BLUR



FOCUS BLUR



Blur should resolve quickly after landing.



\---



\# 54. Directional Blur Transition



Object/camera moves strongly in one direction.



Blur follows that vector.



Next scene continues same direction.



Useful for:



\- slides

\- pans

\- fast cards



\---



\# 55. Zoom Blur Transition



Useful for rapid movement toward/away from subject.



Possible:



PRODUCT

→ rapid push

→ blur peak

→ FEATURE



Use briefly.



\---



\# 56. Focus Transition



Scene A moves out of focus.



Scene B resolves into focus.



Useful for:



\- softer cinematic transitions

\- dimensional environments



Avoid when sharp continuity is more appropriate.



\---



\# 57. Distortion Transition



Possible:



DISPLACEMENT



WARP



RIPPLE



GLITCH



RGB SPLIT



Use only when visual language supports distortion.



\---



\# 58. Displacement Transition



Pixels deform according to field.



Useful for:



\- AI transformation

\- liquid transition

\- abstract morph



Protect brand/UI readability before and after transition.



\---



\# 59. Warp Transition



Scene bends toward destination.



Useful for:



\- portal

\- speed

\- spatial transformation



Destination should feel connected to warp direction.



\---



\# 60. Ripple Transition



Wave spreads from event and reveals next scene.



Useful for:



\- click

\- activation

\- node

\- touch



Can be subtle.



\---



\# 61. Glitch Transition



Use for:



\- error

\- disruption

\- cyber

\- state corruption



Do not use simply because product is technological.



\---



\# 62. Graphic Transition



Use graphic language already present in brand.



Examples:



BRAND SHAPE



LINE



BLOCK



PATTERN



ICON



COLOR FIELD



Useful for maintaining identity.



\---



\# 63. Shape Wipe



Graphic shape crosses frame.



Next scene appears behind it.



Simple and effective when shape belongs to visual system.



\---



\# 64. Line Transition



A line grows or travels and becomes:



divider



path



edge



graph



next-scene structure



Useful for technology and data narratives.



\---



\# 65. Color Field Transition



Brand color fills frame.



Next scene emerges.



Useful for:



\- chapter breaks

\- major structural transitions

\- brand reinforcement



Use sparingly.



\---



\# 66. Brand Transition



Use product identity itself as transition mechanism.



Possible:



LOGO GEOMETRY



BRAND COLOR



ICON



TYPOGRAPHY



SIGNATURE MOTION



This can create a recognizable motion identity.



\---



\# 67. Logo-to-Product



LOGO

→ COMPONENTS SEPARATE

→ UI FORMS



or:



CAMERA ENTERS LOGO

→ PRODUCT WORLD



Useful for opening.



\---



\# 68. Product-to-Logo



PRODUCT

→ COMPONENTS CONVERGE

→ LOGO



Useful for closing.



Strong when geometry is meaningfully related.



\---



\# 69. Typography Transition



Words can connect sections.



Possible:



WORD REPLACEMENT



TEXT PUSH



MASK



SCALE



LETTERFORM MORPH



Use for conceptual or campaign-led films.



\---



\# 70. Word Replacement Transition



Sentence remains.



One key word changes.



Example:



CAPTURE

→ QUALIFY

→ CONVERT



Useful for feature progression.



\---



\# 71. Text Push Transition



Large text moves and physically pushes scene away.



Useful for high-energy brand sequences.



Maintain readability.



\---



\# 72. Transition Direction



Define dominant direction per sequence.



Possible:



LEFT → RIGHT



RIGHT → LEFT



FORWARD



BACKWARD



UP



DOWN



RADIAL



Do not change direction randomly.



\---



\# 73. Forward Direction



Forward movement often communicates:



\- progress

\- discovery

\- entering product

\- deeper detail



Useful default for feature progression.



\---



\# 74. Backward Direction



Pulling backward often communicates:



\- context

\- scale

\- completion

\- ecosystem



Useful for payoff.



\---



\# 75. Vertical Direction



UP can communicate:



\- growth

\- progress

\- elevation



DOWN can communicate:



\- drill-down

\- hierarchy

\- deeper layer



Use according to narrative.



\---



\# 76. Transition Speed



Useful conceptual levels:



SLOW

premium / major reveal



MEDIUM

standard feature change



FAST

montage / energy



INSTANT

hard cut / impact



Transition speed should match shot energy.



\---



\# 77. Transition Duration



Starting ranges:



MICRO

100–250ms



FAST

250–500ms



STANDARD

400–900ms



CINEMATIC

700–1500ms+



Actual duration depends on:



\- distance

\- camera

\- complexity

\- music

\- readability



\---



\# 78. Transition Overlap



Shot A and Shot B can overlap.



Possible:



A exits while B enters.



Useful for:



\- card movement

\- UI navigation

\- crossfades

\- spatial transitions



Overlap can make transitions feel continuous.



\---



\# 79. Transition Hold



Do not begin transition before viewer understands current shot.



Pattern:



REVEAL

→ READ

→ TRANSITION



Not:



REVEAL

→ IMMEDIATE TRANSITION



Information needs time.



\---



\# 80. Transition Landing



Every transition needs a clean landing.



After movement:



SETTLE



ESTABLISH HERO



ALLOW READING



Do not immediately launch another major motion event.



\---



\# 81. Transition + Sound



Possible sound roles:



WHOOSH



RISER



IMPACT



CLICK



SWELL



TONAL SHIFT



SILENCE



Sound should reinforce transition intensity.



\---



\# 82. Sound Bridge



Audio can begin before visual transition.



SHOT A

→ sound of Shot B begins

→ transition

→ Shot B



This can make sequence feel more connected.



\---



\# 83. Audio Tail



Sound from Shot A continues briefly into Shot B.



Useful for softer continuity.



\---



\# 84. Impact Landing



Transition motion resolves with impact sound.



Useful for:



\- product hero

\- logo

\- metric

\- major feature



Reserve stronger impacts for important moments.



\---



\# 85. Silent Transition



Sometimes silence is stronger.



Useful after:



\- dense sequence

\- major statement

\- emotional pause



Not every transition needs a whoosh.



\---



\# 86. Transition + Camera



If camera is already creating continuity,

avoid adding another strong transition effect.



Example:



CAMERA PUSH

\+

PORTAL

may be enough.



Do not automatically add:



blur

\+ flash

\+ RGB split

\+ particles.



\---



\# 87. Transition + UI



Prefer native product movement when available.



Example:



CARD EXPANSION



is usually better than:



CARD

→ random glitch

→ DETAIL.



Product behavior creates credibility.



\---



\# 88. Transition + Typography



When typography carries transition:



reduce other effects.



Text needs clarity.



\---



\# 89. Transition + Data



Follow data when causality matters.



Example:



LEAD

→ DATA PACKET

→ CAMERA FOLLOWS

→ CRM



This explains system behavior while transitioning.



\---



\# 90. Transition Grammar



Define a limited transition grammar per film.



Example:



PRIMARY:

shared element



SECONDARY:

push-through



ACCENT:

occlusion



CUT:

for fast montage



Avoid using every transition family in one video.



\---



\# 91. Signature Transition



A project may have one signature transition.



Examples:



cards expand into scenes



camera travels through nodes



brand shape wipes between chapters



UI layers fold into next interface



Repeat selectively to build identity.



\---



\# 92. Transition Repetition



Repetition can create consistency.



But vary:



scale

direction

speed

context



Do not duplicate exactly every time.



\---



\# 93. Transition Escalation



Transitions can increase in intensity with narrative.



Example:



CUT



→ SHARED ELEMENT



→ PUSH



→ SPATIAL TRAVEL



→ HERO MORPH



Use escalation intentionally.



\---



\# 94. Transition Restraint



A sequence may use:



70% cuts/simple continuity



20% motivated transitions



10% hero transitions



Conceptual guideline only.



The principle:



hero transitions should remain special.



\---



\# 95. Multi-Aspect Transitions



Check transitions in:



16:9



9:16



1:1



A horizontal movement may fail in vertical format.



Prefer transitions that can be re-composed.



\---



\# 96. Mobile-Safe Transition



For vertical video:



\- reduce long horizontal travel

\- keep hero near safe center

\- avoid edge-dependent masks

\- simplify wide spatial moves



Recompose rather than blindly crop.



\---



\# 97. Transition Performance



For browser/Remotion implementations:



prefer the simplest technique capable of producing the visual result.



Possible progression:



CSS transforms



SVG masks



Canvas



WebGL/shaders



Do not use shaders when a transform/mask solves the problem.



\---



\# 98. Transition Anti-Pattern — Random Presets



Problem:



each cut receives a different preset.



Result:



no visual language.



Fix:



define transition grammar.



\---



\# 99. Anti-Pattern — Transition Every Shot



Problem:



viewer watches transitions instead of product.



Fix:



use cuts.



\---



\# 100. Anti-Pattern — No Relationship



Problem:



transition looks impressive but scenes have no visual connection.



Fix:



find:



shape

motion

space

meaning

product behavior



before choosing effect.



\---



\# 101. Anti-Pattern — Endless Zoom



Problem:



every scene pushes into next scene.



Fix:



alternate:



CUT

PUSH

STATIC

PULL

MATCH

OCCLUSION



while maintaining overall grammar.



\---



\# 102. Anti-Pattern — Whip Everything



Problem:



every transition is fast directional blur.



Fix:



reserve whip for high-energy moments.



\---



\# 103. Anti-Pattern — Morph Everything



Problem:



unrelated objects continuously transform.



Fix:



morph only when structural or semantic relationship exists.



\---



\# 104. Anti-Pattern — Effect Stack



Problem:



transition uses:



zoom

\+

blur

\+

flash

\+

glitch

\+

shake



without narrative reason.



Fix:



choose one primary mechanism.



\---



\# 105. Anti-Pattern — No Landing



Problem:



next animation begins before transition settles.



Fix:



TRANSITION

→ LAND

→ READ

→ ACTION



\---



\# 106. Anti-Pattern — Transition Hides Product



Problem:



motion/effect obscures important UI.



Fix:



reduce intensity or transition through non-critical region.

\# 114. Transition Review Checklist



\[ ] Shots have a real relationship



\[ ] Transition mechanism expresses that relationship



\[ ] Direction is intentional



\[ ] Carrying element is clear



\[ ] Product remains readable



\[ ] Duration matches energy



\[ ] Camera does not compete



\[ ] Sound supports intensity



\[ ] Transition lands cleanly



\[ ] Next shot receives reading time



\[ ] Transition belongs to project grammar



\[ ] Same effect is not being overused



\---



\# 115. Sequence Transition Audit



Across the complete film check:



\[ ] Transition families are limited



\[ ] Direction remains coherent



\[ ] Hero transitions remain special



\[ ] Cuts are used confidently



\[ ] No transition exists only to show an effect



\[ ] Spatial relationships make sense



\[ ] UI-native transitions are preferred when appropriate



\[ ] Energy varies



\[ ] Sound transitions are not repetitive



\[ ] Final brand transition feels intentional



\---



\# 116. Creative Director Rule



Never propose:



"Use a cool zoom transition."



Instead define:



FROM:

dashboard overview



TO:

AI qualification feature



RELATIONSHIP:

the feature already exists as a dashboard card



TRANSITION:

camera pushes toward that card while surrounding UI recedes;

the card expands through a shared-element transition and becomes the

full qualification interface.



SOUND:

subtle tonal rise followed by a tactile landing.



The transition must explain why Shot B comes after Shot A.



\---



\# 117. Golden Rule



The best transition is not the one the viewer notices most.



It is the one that makes the next shot feel like the natural consequence

of the previous shot.



Connect ideas first.



Connect objects second.



Connect motion third.



Add effects last.
