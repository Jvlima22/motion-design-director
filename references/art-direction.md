\# Art Direction



\## Purpose



Art direction is the decision layer above animation technique.



Before choosing keyframes, components, effects, camera values, Remotion code,

After Effects expressions, or editing techniques, define how the entire piece

should feel and what visual language it will obey.



The goal is not maximum motion.



The goal is coherent motion.



Good direction often comes from subtraction:

deciding what should NOT move, glow, rotate, bounce, transition, or compete.



\---



\# 1. Direction Before Execution



Always work in this order:



BRIEF

→ MESSAGE

→ AUDIENCE

→ EMOTIONAL INTENT

→ VISUAL DIRECTION

→ MOTION LANGUAGE

→ SHOT SYSTEM

→ IMPLEMENTATION



Never begin with:



"What effect should I use?"



Begin with:



"What should the viewer understand and feel?"

\---



\# 3. Three Direction Pillars



Every project should resolve three questions.



\## Emotional Intent



What should it feel like?



Examples:



\- premium

\- precise

\- powerful

\- calm

\- futuristic

\- intelligent

\- playful

\- trustworthy

\- cinematic



\## Visual Narrative



What is the sequence of understanding?



Example:



PROBLEM

→ PRODUCT

→ INTERACTION

→ TRANSFORMATION

→ RESULT

→ BRAND



\## Motion Craft



How should movement behave?



Examples:



\- smooth

\- sharp

\- physical

\- restrained

\- elastic

\- geometric

\- rhythmic

\- cinematic



These pillars guide every later decision.



\---



\# 4. Tone Matrix



Place the project on two axes:



CALM ←→ KINETIC



SOFT ←→ SHARP



This produces four useful territories.



\## Calm + Soft



Useful for:



\- luxury

\- wellness

\- editorial

\- elegant brands



Characteristics:



\- long easing

\- generous holds

\- minimal stagger

\- subtle movement

\- zero or minimal overshoot



\## Calm + Sharp



Useful for:



\- premium SaaS

\- fintech

\- enterprise software

\- AI products

\- professional technology



Characteristics:



\- precise movement

\- controlled acceleration

\- strong hierarchy

\- clean geometry

\- restrained effects

\- deliberate camera



This should be a strong default for premium SaaS/product films.



\## Kinetic + Soft



Useful for:



\- lifestyle

\- playful products

\- creator tools

\- friendly brands



Characteristics:



\- springs

\- controlled bounce

\- loose timing

\- organic movement



\## Kinetic + Sharp



Useful for:



\- gaming

\- sports

\- hype launches

\- high-energy technology



Characteristics:



\- hard cuts

\- fast acceleration

\- aggressive scale

\- stronger beat synchronization

\- short holds



Do not casually mix several territories.



Contrast can exist between scenes, but the project still needs one dominant

motion personality.



\---



\# 5. Motion Personality



Create one named personality for the project.



Possible starting personalities:



\## Premium



Duration:

350–600ms typical UI movement



Overshoot:

0–2%



Behavior:

smooth, deliberate, elegant



\## Corporate



Duration:

200–400ms



Overshoot:

0–3%



Behavior:

clean, predictable, trustworthy



\## Playful



Duration:

150–300ms



Overshoot:

10–20% when justified



Behavior:

elastic, friendly, expressive



\## Energetic



Duration:

100–250ms



Overshoot:

selected impact moments only



Behavior:

fast, bold, rhythmic



These are starting presets, not immutable rules.

\---



\# 7. Visual Hierarchy



Every frame needs a priority order.



Use:



HERO

→ SUPPORT

→ TEXTURE



Hero:

the reason the shot exists.



Support:

helps explain the hero.



Texture:

creates atmosphere without demanding attention.



If two elements compete equally for attention, reduce one.



Possible hierarchy tools:



1\. size

2\. contrast

3\. color

4\. position

5\. depth

6\. sharpness

7\. movement

8\. lighting



Motion itself is a hierarchy tool.



The fastest or largest movement often becomes the focal point.



Use this intentionally.



\---



\# 8. One Hero Per Beat



A scene can contain many objects.



A beat should normally contain one dominant event.



Examples:



BAD:



headline enters

\+ phone rotates

\+ chart animates

\+ background pulses

\+ particles explode



all simultaneously.



BETTER:



headline establishes context

→ phone becomes hero

→ camera approaches

→ feature activates

→ result becomes hero



The focal point can move through the scene over time.



\---



\# 9. Restraint Pass



Before polishing, ask for every moving element:



Does this movement communicate something?



If not:



consider removing it.



Do not:



\- animate every object

\- glow every accent

\- bounce every entrance

\- rotate every card

\- transition the transition

\- loop text while it is being read

\- keep the camera moving constantly



A static anchor often makes surrounding motion feel more intentional.



\---



\# 10. Signature Moments



Most product films benefit from a limited number of high-intensity moments.



A "wow" moment can be:



\- UI explosion

\- dimensional transformation

\- major camera reveal

\- logo formation

\- dramatic data transformation

\- product ecosystem reveal

\- spatial transition



Do not make every shot the wow moment.



If everything is spectacular, nothing feels special.



Build:



INTRO

→ DEVELOPMENT

→ BUILD

→ PEAK

→ RESOLUTION



\---



\# 11. Composition Philosophy



Composition should guide the eye before motion begins.



Prioritize:



\- focal point

\- hierarchy

\- negative space

\- alignment

\- depth

\- balance

\- safe areas



A premium frame usually feels intentional before any animation is played.



Do not use motion to rescue weak composition.



\---



\# 12. Grid System



For landscape product/video compositions, a useful starting point is:



12 columns



with:



80–120px outer margin at 1920px width



24–32px gutters



Use an 8px baseline rhythm for UI-aligned compositions.



General formula:



content\_width =

frame\_width - 2 × margin



column\_width =

(content\_width - (columns - 1) × gutter) / columns



Treat these as construction tools rather than mandatory aesthetics.



\---



\# 13. Focal Placement



Rule-of-thirds positions are useful starting points:



x ≈ 33% or 67%



y ≈ 33% or 67%



But symmetry can be intentionally used for:



\- product hero shots

\- logos

\- monumental moments

\- stillness

\- formal compositions



Do not apply rule-of-thirds mechanically.



The question is:



Where should the viewer look first?



\---



\# 14. Negative Space



Empty space is active.



Use it to:



\- isolate the hero

\- increase perceived quality

\- create breathing room

\- prepare future motion

\- improve typography

\- create asymmetry



Avoid filling every available region with UI cards or decoration.



Premium does not mean "more."



\---



\# 15. Depth System



Organize spatial scenes into:



BACKGROUND

→ AMBIENT



MIDGROUND

→ SUPPORT



FOREGROUND

→ HERO / DEPTH CUE



Useful parallax starting relationships:



Background:

0.1–0.3×



Midground:

0.5–0.7×



Foreground:

1.0–1.5×



Do not treat these as fixed values.



The principle matters more:



nearer objects should generally respond more strongly than distant objects.



Depth can also be communicated through:



\- blur

\- scale

\- contrast

\- saturation

\- shadow

\- lighting

\- perspective



\---



\# 16. Camera Direction



Every camera move needs a purpose.



\## Push In



Communicates:



\- importance

\- discovery

\- intimacy

\- focus



\## Pull Out



Communicates:



\- context

\- scale

\- ecosystem

\- reveal



\## Pan / Tilt



Communicates:



\- exploration

\- progression

\- spatial discovery



\## Follow



Communicates:



\- continuity

\- action

\- navigation



\## Orbit



Communicates:



\- dimensionality

\- product heroism

\- physical presence



\## Dolly Zoom



Communicates:



\- tension

\- distortion

\- dramatic perception change



Use rarely in clean product presentations.



\---



\# 17. One Camera Intent Per Beat



Avoid casually combining:



PUSH

\+

PAN

\+

ROTATE

\+

ZOOM



Instead decide:



What is the camera doing for this beat?



One clear movement usually reads as more intentional.



Complex camera choreography is allowed when the shot genuinely requires it,

but complexity must be designed rather than accumulated.



\---



\# 18. Camera Timing



Useful starting range for cinematic hero camera movement:



800–2000ms+



Slower movement often feels:



\- more deliberate

\- more cinematic

\- more premium



Fast camera movement can feel:



\- energetic

\- aggressive

\- transitional



Match camera timing to the project's motion personality.



\---



\# 19. Camera and Product Legibility



Never let cinematic perspective make the product unreadable.



For dense UI:



prefer frontal or mild perspective.



For a product hero:



allow stronger perspective.



For feature demonstration:



move toward the relevant interface region.



For text-heavy UI:



prioritize readability over depth spectacle.



The product remains the subject.



\---



\# 20. Enter and Exit Direction



Direction can communicate navigation.



For left-to-right reading systems:



new content often feels natural entering from right/below.



back/undo can reverse established direction.



deeper navigation can use:



scale-up

\+

push-in.



However, do not apply directional conventions blindly.



Product structure and previous shot continuity take priority.



\---



\# 21. Multi-Aspect Direction



Never assume:



16:9 composition

→ crop

→ 9:16 composition



Instead:



RESTACK.



For vertical:



\- reorganize horizontal layouts vertically

\- enlarge important type where necessary

\- reposition hero upward if platform UI occupies the bottom

\- simplify secondary content

\- preserve focal hierarchy



Approximate starting safe areas:



16:9:

\~5% edges



1:1:

\~6–8%



4:5:

\~6–8%



9:16:

larger top/bottom protection because platform UI can overlap content.



Platform interfaces change.



Treat exact percentages as production checks, not timeless constants.



\---



\# 22. Color Direction



Start with a restrained system.



Useful structure:



BASE

\+

PRIMARY / HERO

\+

ACCENT

\+

2–3 NEUTRALS



Avoid introducing hues merely to make the frame more colorful.



Use:



\- lightness

\- chroma

\- contrast

\- depth

\- tint



to create richness.



A hero color should actually behave like a hero.



If everything uses the accent, the accent loses meaning.



\---



\# 23. 60 / 30 / 10 Heuristic



A useful starting distribution:



60% dominant base



30% supporting surfaces



10% hero/accent



Do not treat the percentages mathematically.



The purpose is visual dominance and restraint.



\---



\# 24. Dark Product Films



For premium dark scenes:



avoid:



\- pure black everywhere

\- maximum saturation

\- multiple neon glows

\- uncontrolled bloom



Prefer:



\- subtly tinted blacks

\- restrained accent chroma

\- controlled highlights

\- local glow

\- layered depth



Glow should identify importance, not decorate every object.



\---



\# 25. Color Interpolation



When animating between colors, prefer perceptually uniform spaces when available:



\- OKLCH

\- OKLab

\- Lab



Raw RGB interpolation can produce muddy or visually uneven midpoints.



Evaluate:



\- midpoint saturation

\- lightness continuity

\- banding

\- hue path



\---



\# 26. Gradient Direction



Premium gradients are usually:



\- slow

\- subtle

\- layered

\- text-safe

\- low contrast behind important information



Useful techniques:



\- stacked radial gradients

\- mesh gradients

\- controlled grain

\- slow drift

\- aurora/noise

\- subtle vignette



Fast gradient movement can quickly become screensaver-like.



\---



\# 27. Background Direction



Backgrounds exist to support foreground communication.



Possible levels:



\## Static



Best when:



\- UI is complex

\- typography is dominant

\- motion density is already high



\## Subtle Living Background



Use:



\- slow gradient drift

\- faint light movement

\- minimal particles

\- restrained noise



\## Cinematic Environment



Use when the environment itself participates in storytelling.



Possible techniques:



\- shader

\- volumetric lighting

\- spatial particles

\- dimensional geometry

\- strong parallax



Even cinematic backgrounds must not out-compete the hero.



\---



\# 28. Background Performance



When generating interactive/web backgrounds:



prefer the cheapest technique that achieves the look.



Order:



CSS

→ Canvas

→ WebGL / Shader



For WebGL:



cap device pixel ratio where appropriate.



For continuous backgrounds:



pause when:



\- hidden

\- offscreen

\- reduced-motion is requested



Performance is part of visual quality.



\---



\# 29. Typography Direction



Typography is not an overlay added after animation.



It is part of the composition.



Determine:



\- type family

\- weight

\- scale

\- line height

\- tracking

\- hierarchy

\- alignment

\- reveal behavior



Hero typography should have a clear relationship to the product.



Avoid several competing typographic treatments in one piece.



\---



\# 30. Highlight Strategy



Usually highlight one meaningful element within a headline.



Possible techniques:



\- accent color

\- underline

\- pill

\- weight change

\- mask reveal

\- glow

\- kinetic transformation



Do not highlight several words simultaneously.



Highlighting is hierarchy.



\---



\# 31. Data Direction



Data visualization requires credibility.



For important numbers:



\- animate toward the value

\- decelerate near completion

\- avoid playful overshoot

\- hold the final value

\- reduce competing movement while the value is read



For charts:



preserve:



\- axes

\- relationships

\- scale

\- labels



Do not turn analytical information into decorative noise.



\---



\# 32. Pacing Architecture



Before timing individual keyframes, map energy across the whole video.



Typical structure:



OPEN

→ BUILD

→ PEAK

→ RESOLVE

→ END HOLD



Not every project needs this exact shape.



But every project needs an energy plan.



Pacing is macro-animation.



\---



\# 33. Stillness



Stillness is a creative tool.



Use it after:



\- major reveals

\- numbers

\- feature completion

\- logo formation

\- CTA

\- dramatic transitions



Fast sequence

→ still frame



often creates more perceived impact than adding another effect.



\---



\# 34. Product / SaaS Direction Pattern



For a premium SaaS video:



1\. establish brand world

2\. present product hero

3\. establish problem/context

4\. isolate feature

5\. demonstrate interaction

6\. show response

7\. reveal result

8\. widen into ecosystem/value

9\. brand payoff

10\. CTA



Not every video requires all ten.



Use the sequence as a narrative vocabulary.

\# 36. Consistency Audit



Before implementation or render, check:



\- Is the emotional intent consistent?

\- Is the motion personality consistent?

\- Are comparable movements using compatible easing?

\- Are durations based on a coherent timing system?

\- Is transition vocabulary limited?

\- Is stagger behavior coherent?

\- Is camera language coherent?

\- Is color hierarchy consistent?

\- Is typography consistent?

\- Does every shot have one hero?

\- Are backgrounds subordinate?

\- Are important moments allowed to breathe?



Inconsistency should be intentional.



\---



\# 37. Anti-Patterns



\## Everything Moves



Result:

no focal hierarchy.



Fix:

create static anchors.



\## Everything Glows



Result:

accent loses meaning.



Fix:

reserve glow for focal moments.



\## Everything Is Centered



Result:

composition can feel templated.



Fix:

use intentional asymmetry or deliberate symmetry.



\## Constant Camera Motion



Result:

viewer loses spatial stability.



Fix:

use camera only when narrative benefits.



\## Random Effects



Result:

video feels assembled from presets.



Fix:

define effect vocabulary before production.



\## Blind 16:9 → 9:16 Crop



Result:

broken hierarchy and hidden content.



Fix:

restack.



\## Maximum Saturation



Result:

cheap/neon appearance.



Fix:

control lightness and chroma.



\## No Holds



Result:

viewer never absorbs information.



Fix:

design stillness into the timeline.

\# 38. Creative Director Decision Sequence



When given a product, screen, website, or feature, reason in this order:



WHAT IS THE MESSAGE?

↓

WHO IS WATCHING?

↓

WHAT SHOULD THEY FEEL?

↓

WHAT IS THE HERO?

↓

WHAT VISUAL WORLD FITS?

↓

WHAT MOTION PERSONALITY FITS?

↓

WHAT SHOULD REMAIN STATIC?

↓

HOW SHOULD THE FRAME BE COMPOSED?

↓

DO WE NEED DEPTH?

↓

DO WE NEED CAMERA MOVEMENT?

↓

WHAT IS THE TRANSITION LANGUAGE?

↓

WHAT COLOR SYSTEM SUPPORTS IT?

↓

WHAT TYPOGRAPHIC SYSTEM SUPPORTS IT?

↓

WHAT SOUND SUPPORTS IT?

↓

WHAT IMPLEMENTATION TOOL FITS BEST?



Only then:



HOW DO WE BUILD IT?

\# 40. Golden Rules



Direction precedes technique.



One message is stronger than five.



One hero is stronger than five competing focal points.



Consistency creates confidence.



Restraint creates perceived quality.



Negative space is part of the design.



Stillness is part of motion.



Camera movement needs narrative purpose.



Color needs hierarchy.



Typography needs hierarchy.



Backgrounds support; they do not compete.



Do not use effects to compensate for weak composition.



Do not use motion to compensate for weak storytelling.



Do not use cinematic style to hide the product.



Every creative decision must support:



MESSAGE

\+

HIERARCHY

\+

EMOTION

\+

COMPREHENSION

\+

BRAND
