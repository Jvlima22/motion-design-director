# Product Video Master Workflow

## Purpose

This workflow is the master production system for product-focused motion design.

Use it for:

- software product films
- SaaS videos
- application launches
- feature launches
- product demonstrations
- interface-driven motion
- technology product films
- social adaptations of product videos

This workflow defines HOW a product-video project moves from evidence to final delivery.

It does not replace:

- art direction
- composition
- typography
- shot vocabulary
- component patterns
- transitions
- effects
- audio design
- implementation-engine rules
- quality-control references

It orchestrates them.

---

# Core Principle

A product video is not a collection of attractive scenes.

It is a sequence of communication decisions.

Every scene must answer:

WHAT MUST THE VIEWER UNDERSTAND, FEEL OR BELIEVE NEXT?

Then choose the visual and audiovisual solution.

Never reverse this process.

Do not search for a cool effect and invent a reason to use it.

BRIEF

# 1. Brief Sufficiency Gate

Before asking questions, inspect all information already available.

Possible evidence includes:

- user brief
- product URL
- screenshots
- screen recordings
- existing product video
- brand guide
- logo
- design system
- Figma
- application build
- product documentation
- supplied copy
- previous approved work

Ask only for information whose absence materially blocks the next decision.

Do not ask questions merely because a template says to ask them.

If sufficient evidence exists:

PROCEED.

If important evidence is missing but safe assumptions are possible:

STATE THE ASSUMPTION
-> PROCEED

If missing information could cause false product claims or major rework:

ASK.

---

# 2. Product Truth

Product truth precedes spectacle.

Before representing a capability, determine whether the available evidence supports it.

Classify important product claims as:

VERIFIED
SUPPORTED
INFERRED
UNKNOWN

VERIFIED:
directly visible or explicitly confirmed.

SUPPORTED:
strongly supported by supplied product material.

INFERRED:
reasonable interpretation but not directly established.

UNKNOWN:
insufficient evidence.

Do not visually present INFERRED or UNKNOWN functionality as confirmed product behavior.

If a concept visualization is intentionally speculative, label it internally as such.

Never invent:

- dashboards
- metrics
- AI capabilities
- integrations
- analytics
- notifications
- workflows
- health metrics
- financial results
- customer results
- product interactions

merely because they make the video more impressive.

PRODUCT TRUTH > VISUAL SPECTACLE.

---

# 3. Audience

Identify who must understand the video.

Examples:

- potential customer
- existing customer
- investor
- technical buyer
- end user
- enterprise decision-maker
- social audience
- app-store visitor
- internal stakeholder

Determine:

WHAT DO THEY ALREADY KNOW?

WHAT DO THEY NEED TO UNDERSTAND?

WHAT WOULD MAKE THEM CARE?

WHAT WOULD MAKE THEM DISTRUST THE VIDEO?

Audience determines information density, terminology, proof and pacing.

---

# 4. Communication Goal

Define one primary communication goal.

Examples:

- introduce a product
- create desire
- explain a workflow
- demonstrate a feature
- communicate differentiation
- prove simplicity
- show transformation
- establish premium positioning
- drive conversion
- announce a launch

Secondary goals may exist.

They must not compete with the primary goal.

Write:

PRIMARY GOAL:
SECONDARY GOAL:
DESIRED VIEWER RESPONSE:

# 5. Deliverable Context

Determine the viewing environment before designing scenes.

Record:

FORMAT:
ASPECT RATIO:
TARGET DURATION:
PLATFORM:
EXPECTED VIEWING SIZE:
SOUND EXPECTATION:
LOOPING:
CTA REQUIREMENT:
DELIVERY CONSTRAINTS:

Do not assume 1920x1080.

Do not assume 30 FPS.

Do not assume landscape.

Do not assume sound will be enabled.

Platform context may alter:

- composition
- typography
- pacing
- shot duration
- safe zones
- copy density
- CTA behavior
- audio strategy

---

# 6. Brand / Visual Evidence

Extract visual evidence from supplied material before creating fallback styling.

Inspect:

- background behavior
- primary colors
- accent colors
- typography
- border radius
- shadows
- icon language
- spacing
- UI density
- illustration style
- photographic style
- motion behavior
- tone

Separate:

OBSERVED BRAND LANGUAGE

from:

PROPOSED MOTION LANGUAGE

Do not mistake personal aesthetic preference for product identity.

When brand information is incomplete, create a restrained neutral system that can be replaced without restructuring the production.

---

# 7. Core Message

Reduce the video to one sentence.

If the core message cannot be stated clearly, the scene plan is premature.

Template:

THIS VIDEO SHOULD MAKE [AUDIENCE]
UNDERSTAND THAT [PRODUCT TRUTH / VALUE]
SO THAT THEY [DESIRED RESPONSE].

Then identify:

HERO IDEA:
SUPPORTING IDEA 1:
SUPPORTING IDEA 2:
OPTIONAL PROOF:

Avoid giving equal visual weight to every feature.

---

# 8. Narrative Strategy

Do not force every product video into one narrative arc.

Select the strategy that best solves the communication problem.

Possible strategies include:

## Problem -> Solution -> Proof

Useful when the pain is important to understanding the product.

## Transformation

BEFORE
-> CHANGE
-> AFTER

Useful when improvement is the core promise.

## Feature Journey

Move through a small number of capabilities in a deliberate progression.

Useful for product overviews.

## Product Reveal

CURIOSITY
-> REVEAL
-> EXPLORATION
-> RESOLUTION

Useful for launches and premium films.

## Task Completion

GOAL
-> INTERACTION
-> RESULT

Useful for demos and workflow explanation.

## Before -> After

Direct contrast between old and new states.

## Brand Manifesto

BELIEF
-> TENSION
-> VISION
-> PRODUCT AS EXPRESSION

Useful when positioning matters more than detailed explanation.

## Hybrid

Combine strategies only when each section has a clear purpose.

Never combine narrative structures merely to include more ideas.

---

# 9. Information Priority

Classify information before creating scenes.

LEVEL 1 - MUST UNDERSTAND

The video fails if the viewer misses it.

LEVEL 2 - SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Strengthens the message.

LEVEL 3 - NICE TO KNOW

Include only if time and clarity allow.

When duration becomes constrained:

REMOVE LEVEL 3 FIRST.

Then simplify LEVEL 2.

Protect LEVEL 1.

Do not solve information overload by shrinking typography.

---

# 10. Scene Architecture

Scenes are communication units, not containers for effects.

For every scene define:

SCENE PURPOSE:
MESSAGE:
VIEWER STATE BEFORE:
VIEWER STATE AFTER:
HERO:
SUPPORTING INFORMATION:
ESTIMATED DURATION:
ENTRY LOGIC:
EXIT LOGIC:

Scene count follows communication needs and duration.

Do not use a fixed duration-to-scene-count table as law.

A scene may be long when comprehension requires it.

A scene may be extremely short when it functions as punctuation.

---

# 11. Copy Plan

Write the essential copy before final animation.

For each text element ask:

DOES THE VIEWER NEED TO READ THIS?

CAN THE VISUAL COMMUNICATE IT BETTER?

IS IT PRODUCT TRUTH?

IS IT DUPLICATING ANOTHER MESSAGE?

Estimate readability using:

- word count
- reading time
- display duration
- hierarchy
- movement
- viewing size
- platform

Do not impose arbitrary word counts when comprehension can be measured more directly.

Prefer:

ONE PRIMARY IDEA PER MOMENT.

---

# 12. Asset Plan

Inventory assets before implementation.

Classify each asset:

AVAILABLE
NEEDS EXTRACTION
NEEDS CREATION
NEEDS RECREATION
OPTIONAL
BLOCKED

Possible assets:

- logo
- screenshots
- UI recordings
- device mockups
- icons
- photos
- video
- illustrations
- charts
- product data
- music
- SFX
- fonts

Never silently substitute an invented product screen for a missing real screen.

For every important shot define:

HERO:
SHOT TYPE:
COMPONENT PATTERN:
COMPOSITION:
CAMERA:
PRIMARY MOTION:
SECONDARY MOTION:
TYPOGRAPHY:
TRANSITION IN:
TRANSITION OUT:
EFFECTS:
SOUND STRATEGY:
MUSIC / BEAT RELATIONSHIP:
SFX LANGUAGE:
AUDIO-MOTION SYNC:
DURATION:
IMPLEMENTATION ENGINE:
VALIDATION TARGET:

Do not fill fields that are irrelevant.

The contract exists to force decisions, not bureaucracy.

---

# 14. Composition Fit Gate

Before polishing a scene, verify that the composition fits the communication.

Check:

- visual hierarchy
- hero dominance
- useful frame occupancy
- negative space
- readability
- balance
- safe zones
- UI legibility
- format adaptation

COMMUNICATION FIT FIRST.

COMPOSITION SERVES COMMUNICATION.

AESTHETICS REFINES THE SOLUTION.

Do not fix empty composition by adding unnecessary information.

Do not fix information overload by shrinking everything.

Change the composition.

# 16. Audiovisual Strategy

Audio is optional.

Audiovisual thinking is not.

Determine whether the project needs:

- music
- SFX
- interface feedback
- ambience
- voice
- silence as contrast
- beat relationship
- precise audio-motion synchronization

Route according to the actual decision:

WHY / WHERE SOUND
-> references/audio/sound-design.md

WHEN RELATIVE TO MUSIC
-> references/audio/beat-sync.md

WHAT SOUND
-> references/audio/sfx-language.md

HOW SOUND BINDS TO MOTION
-> references/audio/audio-motion-sync.md

For existing media analysis or synchronization verification:

-> references/quality/audio-analysis.md

Do not load all audio modules by default.

If music is structurally important, rhythm may influence scene and shot timing before final animation.

Do not postpone structural audio decisions until the end of production.

# 17. Implementation Plan

Only now select implementation engines.

Possible engines include:

REMOTION
AFTER EFFECTS
DAVINCI RESOLVE
HYBRID

Choose according to:

- required motion
- existing assets
- iteration speed
- automation needs
- UI fidelity
- compositing complexity
- editing requirements
- finishing requirements
- delivery environment

The product-video workflow must remain tool-agnostic.

Implementation-specific rules belong to their implementation modules.

SAAS IS A PRODUCT CONTEXT.

REMOTION IS AN IMPLEMENTATION ENGINE.

Do not confuse them.

# 18. Production Order

Recommended production logic:

PRODUCT EVIDENCE
-> NARRATIVE
-> SCENE PLAN
-> COPY
-> ASSETS
-> SHOT PLAN
-> STRUCTURAL TIMING
-> VISUAL BLOCKING
-> MOTION
-> AUDIO IMPLEMENTATION, WHEN REQUIRED
-> TRANSITIONS
-> POLISH
-> FINAL TIMING
-> REVIEW
-> DELIVERY

This order is adaptive.

For music-driven work:

MUSIC STRUCTURE
may influence
STRUCTURAL TIMING
before detailed motion.

For interaction-driven demos:

PRODUCT INTERACTION
may determine
SHOT TIMING
before decorative motion.

Do not treat production order as a rigid waterfall.

---

# 19. Scene Verification Gate

Before considering a scene finished, verify:

COMMUNICATION
- purpose is clear
- one dominant idea exists
- product claims are supported

COMPOSITION
- hero is obvious
- hierarchy works
- frame occupancy is intentional
- text remains readable

MOTION
- movement has purpose
- primary and secondary motion are distinguishable
- easing supports behavior
- entry and exit are coherent

CONTINUITY
- scene belongs to the surrounding sequence
- transition supports the relationship between scenes

AUDIO, WHEN RELEVANT
- audio event has a reason
- causality is coherent
- timing is deliberate
- unsupported perceptual claims are avoided

TECHNICAL
- implementation-specific validation passes

If a scene fails, fix the cause.

Do not hide structural problems with effects.

---

# 20. Sequence Review

After individual scenes pass, review the video as a sequence.

Check:

- narrative clarity
- pacing
- repetition
- escalation
- contrast
- visual continuity
- transition logic
- information retention
- product truth
- brand consistency
- audiovisual coherence
- ending strength

A collection of good scenes can still make a bad video.

The sequence is the product.

---

# 21. Final Review

Review the final rendered artifact whenever possible.

Do not validate only source code or isolated frames.

Inspect:

VISUAL
AUDIO
TIMING
PRODUCT TRUTH
TECHNICAL DELIVERY

When audio cannot actually be perceived, separate:

MEASURED
OBSERVED
INFERRED
NOT_VERIFIED

Measurement does not become listening.

---

# 22. Delivery

Delivery requirements come from destination, not habit.

Validate:

- dimensions
- aspect ratio
- frame rate
- duration
- codec
- container
- bitrate / quality
- audio presence
- loudness when relevant
- file size
- platform constraints
- final frame behavior
- loop behavior when relevant

Do not assume one universal delivery preset.

# Anti-Patterns

Do not:

- start with effects
- start with implementation code
- invent product capabilities
- treat every feature equally
- force every video into one narrative arc
- use fixed scene counts as universal law
- shrink typography to fit excessive copy
- fill empty space with unnecessary information
- assume landscape
- assume 30 FPS
- assume dark mode
- assume music
- assume Remotion
- postpone structural rhythm decisions when music drives the piece
- confuse mathematical audio proximity with musical intention
- judge final audiovisual quality only from source code
- use polish to hide weak communication

# Master Decision Loop

WHAT IS TRUE?
-> WHO NEEDS TO UNDERSTAND IT?
-> WHAT MUST THEY UNDERSTAND?
-> IN WHAT VIEWING CONTEXT?
-> WHAT IS THE CORE MESSAGE?
-> WHAT NARRATIVE STRATEGY SERVES IT?
-> WHAT INFORMATION DESERVES PRIORITY?
-> WHAT SCENES COMMUNICATE IT?
-> WHAT SHOTS SOLVE THOSE SCENES?
-> WHAT VISUAL LANGUAGE SERVES THE SHOTS?
-> WHAT AUDIOVISUAL LANGUAGE IS REQUIRED?
-> WHAT ENGINE SHOULD IMPLEMENT IT?
-> DOES THE RENDER COMMUNICATE THE INTENDED RESULT?

If the answer fails at any stage:

RETURN TO THE EARLIEST WRONG DECISION.

Do not compensate downstream for an upstream mistake.

---

# Golden Rule

DO NOT BUILD THE VIDEO THE PRODUCT LOOKS COOL IN.

BUILD THE VIDEO THAT MAKES THE RIGHT PRODUCT TRUTH CLEAR, DESIRABLE AND MEMORABLE.
