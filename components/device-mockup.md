\# Device Mockup



\## Purpose



Present a digital product inside a recognizable physical device while using

the device as a storytelling, composition and transition tool.



A device mockup is not merely:



SCREENSHOT

\+

PHONE FRAME.



It can communicate:



CONTEXT



PLATFORM



SCALE



REALISM



PREMIUM PRESENTATION



PHYSICALITY



TRANSITION BETWEEN PRODUCT AND INTERFACE



The device must support the product story.



\---



\# 1. When to Use



Use Device Mockup for:



\- mobile applications

\- wearable applications

\- desktop software

\- responsive products

\- multi-device ecosystems

\- app launch films

\- product hero shots

\- platform demonstrations

\- UI-to-real-world transitions

\- app store / marketing sequences



\---



\# 2. When Not to Use



Avoid device mockups when:



\- device adds no useful context

\- interface itself is more important

\- small screen reduces readability

\- sequence already established platform

\- product is primarily browser-based and device body adds clutter

\- mockup makes product feel like generic template advertising



Sometimes full-screen UI is stronger.



\---

\# 121. Creative Director Rule



Do not say:



"Put the app inside a rotating phone."



Define:



ROLE:

phone establishes that the product is mobile.



ENTRY:

device begins in three-quarter silhouette.



LIGHT:

brand-colored rim light reveals the edge.



MOTION:

phone rotates only until the screen becomes readable.



SCREEN:

activity dashboard activates.



CAMERA:

after a short hold, camera pushes toward the display.



TRANSITION:

screen expands until bezel leaves frame.



NEXT SHOT:

full-screen activity interface.



Now the device establishes context and becomes a transition,

instead of functioning as decoration.



\---



\# 122. Golden Rule



The device is not the product.



The device is the stage on which the product appears.



Use it to establish:



WHERE THE PRODUCT LIVES



HOW IT IS EXPERIENCED



HOW THE VIEWER ENTERS THE INTERFACE



Then remove the device when it no longer helps communication.



PRODUCT FIRST.



DEVICE SECOND.
